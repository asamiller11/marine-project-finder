About this prototype

Public permit and environmental assessment notices for marine construction projects likely to need habitat work

Last updated: October 29, 2026

ProjectsAbout

What it does

The tool reads public environmental assessment and permit notices for marine construction projects, such as ports, wharves, breakwaters, dredging and shoreline works. It flags projects that may need marine habitat work, for example avoidance and reduction measures, habitat enhancement, fish habitat offsetting, or nature-inclusive design. Each listing links to the official notice.

What the score and tiers mean

Each notice gets a score from 0 to 100 from simple, visible rules: the type of marine work, words about fish habitat, offsetting, eelgrass, shellfish or species at risk, and the size of the work. The score places each notice in a tier:

  • Strong match: Score 60 or more. Several signals of habitat work, such as offsetting, new habitat, or large in-water works.
  • Possible: Score 35 to 59. Some signals. Worth a look.
  • Weak: Score 20 to 34. Few signals. Often routine maintenance.

Notices scoring under 20, quarries and pits, and withdrawn projects are not listed at all. Only Strong and Possible matches are shown at first; Weak matches can be switched on with the "Show weaker matches" toggle. A high score means the notice is worth a closer look. It does not mean that mitigation or compensation is required. Only the regulator decides that.

The short quotes on each listing are complete sentences copied exactly from the notice, chosen because they describe the project and its habitat signals, so you can judge for yourself.

Automatic listings and review

Every scored notice is published automatically, marked "Automatic, not yet reviewed". When a person has checked a listing against the official notice, it is marked "Reviewed by a person". Listings found to be wrong or irrelevant on review are removed. Summaries are kept short and factual, and facts that a notice does not give are shown as "unknown".

When the same project appears in two registries, it is listed once, with a link to the other listing. Listings whose official page cannot be found are left out.

Notices in other languages

Quotes always stay in the notice's original language. There is no automatic translation. For notices not in English, the English summary is built only from the keywords and sizes the tool found in the text, and is labelled "built from keywords, not a translation". When too little was found, the listing says "Not translated" and shows the original-language quotes instead. The keyword rules for French, Danish, Spanish, Norwegian and Dutch have fewer tests than the English rules, so those listings are lower confidence and carry a "Less tested language" badge.

Project watch

Some sources list projects rather than notices. When such a source is used, its items appear in a separate "Project watch (not a notice feed)" section, with no comment deadline, no countdown, and never in the closing-soon list. No project watch source is in use at the moment.

Coverage

For now the site is mostly Canada: 23 of the 30 listed notices come from Canadian sources. Other countries have one source each or none yet, so their lists are short or empty.

"Covered" means a source for that place is read automatically. "Partial" means only some sources are read, and they are named. "Coming soon" means no source is read yet, and no listings are shown for it.

How often it updates

The list is refreshed every 2 weeks, and new notices appear on their own. Comment countdowns are calculated in your browser, so they stay current between updates.

Known limits

  • Public notices often appear after a project's design is done, so some listings will be too late to influence the design.
  • Automatic listings have not been checked by a person and can be wrong.
  • Some size readings can be wrong, for example when a notice also mentions a road or pipeline length.
  • Many countries and regions are not covered yet; see the coverage table above.
  • Notices are quoted in their original language. Keyword rules in languages other than English have fewer tests, so those listings are lower confidence.
