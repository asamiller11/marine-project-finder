What it does

The tool reads public environmental assessment and permit notices for marine construction projects in Canada, such as ports, wharves, breakwaters, dredging and shoreline works. It flags projects that may need marine habitat work, for example avoidance and reduction measures, habitat enhancement, fish habitat offsetting, or nature-inclusive design. Each listing links to the official notice.

What the score and confidence mean

Each notice gets a score from 0 to 100 from simple, visible rules: the type of marine work, words about fish habitat, offsetting, eelgrass, shellfish or species at risk, and the size of the work. The confidence label (High, Medium or Low) sums this up and is checked by a person. A high score means the notice is worth a closer look. It does not mean that mitigation or compensation is required. Only the regulator decides that.

The short quotes on each listing are the exact sentences from the notice that led to the flag, so you can judge for yourself.

How items are reviewed

A person reads every candidate before it appears here. Items that are not reviewed and approved stay off this site. Summaries are kept short and factual, and facts that a notice does not give are shown as "unknown".

Public sources used

More provinces and countries may be added later.

How often it updates

The list is refreshed every 2 weeks. Comment countdowns are calculated in your browser, so they stay current between updates.

Known limits

  • Public notices often appear after a project's design is done, so some listings will be too late to influence the design.
  • Some size readings can be wrong, for example when a notice also mentions a road or pipeline length.
  • Not every province is covered yet.
  • Notices in French are quoted in French.
